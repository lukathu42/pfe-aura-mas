# F4 — "Multi-agent video surveillance" as an idea predates deep learning by two decades (Monitorix, 2000)

**Claim**: Abreu et al.'s Monitorix (IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Conference 2000) is a fully decentralized, FIPA-standard multi-agent traffic-surveillance system: real ACL messaging, BDI-like production-system reasoning per agent, a four-tier role-specialized agent taxonomy, a "Proxy" middle-agent pattern bridging a classical CV pipeline (VOK) into the agent society, and a Tracker agent doing cross-camera vehicle re-identification via an online-adapted traffic model.

**Evidence**: sources/11_monitorix-multi-agent-traffic-surveillance.md, verbatim quotes from abstract and introduction.

**Implication**: Combined with [[F2_bousetouane_strongest_local_precedent]], this establishes that "agents cooperate to surveil a place" (the user's original, pre-modernization theme wording) is not a novel framing at any level — local or international — and dates to at least 2000. A defensible thesis cannot rest novelty on "using multiple agents for surveillance" per se. It must rest on a specific, named mechanism (e.g., decision/generation-decoupled LLM explanation grounded in evidence, or an auditable evaluation methodology for coordination overhead) that these classical-era systems did not and could not have.

**Confidence**: High (paper read in full, 6 pages).
