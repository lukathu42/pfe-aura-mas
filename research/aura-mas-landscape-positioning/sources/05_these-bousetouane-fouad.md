# Traitement décentralisé des informations visuelles dans les réseaux de capteurs sans fil multimédia (Distributed Multi-object Tracking and Re-identification across Camera Networks with Disjoint Views)

## Bibliographic metadata

- **Title (FR, cover page)**: "Traitement décentralisé des informations visuelles dans les réseaux de capteurs sans fil multimédia"
- **Title (EN, thesis's own translation used for the abstract/body)**: "Distributed Multi-object Tracking and re-identification across camera networks with disjoint views"
- **Author(s)**: Fouad Bousetouane
- **Year**: 2015
- **Institution / Degree type**: Université Badji Mokhtar – Annaba, Faculté des Sciences de l'Ingénieur, Département d'Informatique. **PhD thesis** ("Thèse pour obtenir le diplôme de Docteur 3ème cycle"), Filière Informatique, Spécialité "Reconnaissance de Formes et Intelligence Artificielle" (Pattern Recognition and Artificial Intelligence).
- **Supervisor**: Directrice de thèse: Dib Lynda, Professeur, Université d'Annaba. - **Length**: ~153 pages of substantive content (6 chapters + bibliography starting at printed p.147), i.e. a full PhD-length thesis. Note: PDF physical page numbers are offset by +6 relative to the thesis's own printed page numbers throughout (front matter accounts for the gap).

## Research problem (in the document's own terms)

The thesis addresses **multi-object tracking and re-identification across a network of cameras with non-overlapping (disjoint) fields of view**, in a setting with no central processing unit, no camera calibration, no fixed/known network topology, and wireless (bandwidth-limited) inter-camera communication. The core problem: when an object leaves the field of view of one camera and later appears in another camera's field of view, the system must determine — without any centralized authority — whether this is the same object already tracked elsewhere in the network (re-identification), and must do so under conditions of highly variable inter-camera lighting/color, unknown entry/exit points, dynamic (ad-hoc) network topology, and real-time constraints. The author explicitly criticizes prior work (Javed 2008a, Chen 2011, etc.) for relying on centralized architectures, fixed/closed network topologies, camera calibration, or offline learning of entry/exit links — restrictions the thesis sets out to remove entirely.

## Objectives

1. Improve mono-camera building blocks (moving-object detection, feature extraction/target representation, intra-camera tracking) so they are robust enough to serve as the basis of multi-camera tracking.
2. Propose a **fully decentralized** system for re-identification and tracking of moving objects across a disjoint-view, ad-hoc wireless camera network, with no restrictions on network topology, camera count, calibration, or entry/exit points.
3. Model the camera network using the **multi-agent paradigm**: each camera is an autonomous "cognitive agent" ("caméra intelligente") that performs local detection/tracking/representation and collaborates with other camera-agents via a consensus protocol to agree on object identities.
4. Achieve real-time performance and demonstrate robustness in real, complex (uncontrolled) environments — not just constrained lab benchmarks.

## Proposed approach (high-level)

A generic "cognitive agent" model is defined and instantiated once per physical camera in the network. Each camera-agent runs five internal modules in sequence: (1) moving-object detection (Gaussian Mixture Model background modeling + post-processing + connected-component labeling); (2) inter-camera color adaptation via a Mean Brightness Transfer Function (MBTF) computed offline per camera pair; (3) feature extraction and target representation (a proposed mixture of low-level statistical moments and Haralick co-occurrence/texture-based contextual features, designed to be invariant across cameras and discriminant across objects); (4) a novel **consensus-based, fully distributed re-identification algorithm** — the thesis's central contribution — in which a camera that detects a new object broadcasts its (color-adapted) feature vector to neighboring camera-agents, receives replies, and either adopts an existing identity (if a neighbor recognizes the object) or mints a new global ID; (5) intra-camera tracking via a proposed adaptive particle filter with mixed state ("APF-MC") that also carries an object-identity label in its state vector so that after re-identification the correct object index is tracked. No central server, no camera calibration, and no assumption about network topology or the number of nodes is used anywhere in the pipeline.

## System architecture

**Explicitly and substantively multi-agent** — this is one of the few surveyed local theses that frames its architecture in genuine agent terms rather than loosely borrowing the word.

- **Topology**: modeled as a graph G=(V,E) where V = the set of Nc cameras and E ⊂ (V×V) the (arbitrary, dynamic) communication edges; no fixed topology assumed, cameras can be added/removed/repositioned at runtime.
- **Agent model** ("Modèle générique d'agent cognitif"): each camera is a physically separate compute unit (own CPU/RAM, own OS) running an *instance* of the same generic agent model. Communication is wireless, point-to-point, with no shared/central data store. Each agent maintains its own local dataset ("Table de données T") logging communication/identity history.
- **Per-agent internal pipeline** (Fig. 5.1/5.2 in the thesis): Détection d'objets mobiles → Ré-identification des cibles inter-caméra ↔ {Extraction des caractéristiques et représentation des cibles, Adaptation de la couleur inter-caméra} → Suivi d'objets mobiles intra-caméra. The re-identification module is the "trigger" of collaboration between agents.
- **Coordination mechanism**: an explicit **consensus algorithm** (the thesis's own contribution, "Consensus Intelligent" / "Algorithme proposé basé sur le Consensus", Fig. 5.7): when camera Cam_i detects a new observation, it computes color-transfer functions to its known neighbors (Sub_C, the subset of cameras that have already detected an object — treated as neighbors), builds the feature vector, and broadcasts a message M = (feature vector, camera ID) to those neighbors. Each neighbor replies with (its last-known ID for a matching object, its own camera ID, a boolean "already seen" flag). If any neighbor confirms a match (Bool=True), the object inherits that identity; otherwise a fresh global identity is minted as Max(neighbor IDs)+1. This is a genuine decentralized decision procedure, not a single global classifier — each agent decides locally and only relies on message-passing with neighbors, explicitly citing multi-agent-systems literature (Soto 2009, Olfati-Saber 2007) for the "consensus" concept.
- The thesis explicitly states this is architecturally different from — and a response to — the centralized architectures dominant in prior inter-camera re-ID literature (Javed 2008a, Chen 2011), and claims (to its own knowledge) no fully decentralized solution had been proposed before for this problem.
- Practical deployment: a real 7-camera ad-hoc wireless testbed ("E-CAM-1" through "E-CAM-7", three camera models: Sony, Sony-G, Sony-G-lens) was physically built at the university's Computer Science department, each camera wired to a separate machine (an HP laptop, a Packard Bell netbook, and a MacBook Air), running Ubuntu/Mac-OS-X, communicating via the YARP (Yet Another Robot Platform) middleware, with OpenCV 2.4 for the vision modules, implemented in C++.

## AI/ML techniques used

- **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)** for statistical background modeling / foreground segmentation, with a proposed Gaussian-kernel convolution post-processing step to suppress shadows/vibration/jitter, followed by connected-component (union-find style) labeling.
- **Particle filter** (Sequential Monte Carlo / recursive Bayesian filtering), extended into a proposed "Adaptive Particle Filter with Mixed-State" (APF-MC): adapts each particle's search-space to the target's real (estimated) size to handle scale change/non-rigid motion, and integrates a mixture of visual cues into the likelihood/importance model to reduce false local minima. The particle state is later extended to also carry an object-identity label for multi-object tracking with several filter instances running in parallel (one per tracked object).
- **Consensus-based distributed inference algorithm** (the thesis's original contribution) — a rule-based, message-passing decision procedure for identity agreement across agents; not a learned classifier.
- Distance/similarity measures used for matching/representation: **Bhattacharyya distance**, **Kullback-Leibler divergence**, and **Mahalanobis distance** (used for intra-camera identification against the stored feature table).
- No deep learning / CNNs anywhere in the thesis (consistent with its 2015 timeframe) — all detection, representation, and matching are classical statistical/probabilistic computer-vision techniques.

## Computer vision techniques

- **Moving-object detection**: GMM-based background subtraction, thresholded/binarized subtraction map, Gaussian-kernel post-filter, connected-component grouping/labeling to produce a "carte de soustraction binarisée et étiquetée."
- **Target representation / feature extraction**: a custom mixture of low-level statistical moments (order-1, spatial distribution) and **Haralick co-occurrence texture features**, engineered to be invariant intra/inter-camera and discriminant inter-object (author's own prior publications: Bousetouane 2012, 2013b are cited as sources for this contribution).
- **Inter-camera color adaptation**: **Mean Brightness Transfer Function (MBTF)**, computed per camera pair from paired observations of the same objects, mapping color/brightness distributions between camera color histograms (an established technique from Javed 2005/2008a/b and Prosser 2008, adopted rather than invented here, but engineered into the fully decentralized pipeline).
- **Intra-camera tracking**: the proposed adaptive mixed-state particle filter (APF-MC), compared empirically against a classical color-based particle filter, color-based Mean-Shift, and contextual Mean-Shift.
- **Re-identification / inter-camera matching**: histogram-based signature comparison using the adapted-color feature vectors + Haralick contextual descriptors, with Mahalanobis-distance-based intra-camera identity verification.

## Agent-based, multi-agent, distributed, or coordination components

**Explicit and central — this is the defining contribution of the thesis.** The system is architected from the ground up as a multi-agent system: a generic "cognitive agent" model is proposed and instantiated once per camera; agents are described as "autonomous in their local decision and processing"; a formal consensus algorithm governs inter-agent collaboration to reach agreement on object identities across the network; the thesis situates itself explicitly within "intelligence artificielle distribuée" (distributed AI) and multi-agent-systems literature (citing Soto 2009, Olfati-Saber 2007, Hu 2010, Quaritsch 2007, and the author's own prior consensus paper, Bousetouane 2013c). This is a decision-level, rule-based multi-agent coordination system (not an LLM-based or planning/reasoning agent in the modern "agentic AI" sense) — but it is a genuine instance of autonomous, cooperating, decision-making camera nodes, which is architecturally rare among the surveyed local theses (most others are single-module pipelines that use "system" loosely).

## Dataset(s) used

- **Custom/collected**: the primary evaluation dataset is video captured from the author's own 7-camera ad-hoc wireless testbed deployed at the university (E-CAM-1 through E-CAM-7), across 4 designed scenarios with 6 distinct human subjects walking varied, unconstrained paths through disjoint camera fields of view (with deliberately induced complex conditions: occlusion, non-rigid motion, scale change, inter-camera illumination variation, cluttered/dynamic background). Not a public benchmark for the inter-camera re-ID chapter.
- **Public benchmark datasets** were used for the earlier, mono-camera tracking-only evaluation chapters (Chapter 4): CAVIAR (2004), SPEVI (2007) — including "motinas-Room160" sequence used for the discrepancy-based position-error evaluation — and PETS2006/PETS2009 (an 8-person outdoor sequence with heavy occlusion).
- All inter-camera MBTF color-transfer functions were computed **offline** ("Hors-ligne") as a one-time calibration step per camera pair, but all detection/tracking/re-identification experiments were run **online** in real time.

## Evaluation methodology and metrics

- **Chapter 4 (intra-camera tracking)**: discrepancy-method evaluation (Schlogl 2004) — Euclidean distance between the algorithm's estimated trajectory and manually annotated ground-truth positions, reported as a percentage position-estimation error over time, benchmarked against classical color-based particle filter, color-based Mean-Shift, and contextual Mean-Shift.
- **Chapter 5 (inter-camera re-identification, the core contribution)**: standard binary-classification evaluation framework — **ROC curves** (True-Positive-Rate vs False-Positive-Rate) and **Precision–Recall curves**, computed by varying a re-identification confidence threshold over time, sampled every 10 frames across 400-frame sequences per camera, across the 4 real-world multi-camera scenarios. VP/FP/FN/VN (TP/FP/FN/TN) are explicitly defined in re-identification terms (e.g., correctly assigning an existing ID = true positive; correctly assigning a new ID to a genuinely new object = true positive; wrongly assigning an existing ID to a different object = false positive).

## Main quantitative results

- **Mean inter-camera re-identification rate (TRM)** across the 4 tested scenarios: **90.11%** (computed as the average True-Positive-Rate across the 4 scenarios' ROC curves, thesis eq. 5.10).
- ROC curves (Fig. 5.12) show TPR consistently well above the diagonal / above the FPR across all 4 scenarios, and Precision–Recall curves (Fig. 5.13) show precision remaining close to 1.0 across most of the recall range, dropping sharply only near recall≈1.0.
- Qualitative tracking-robustness demonstrations: correct re-identification maintained despite partial occlusion (frame 1169, E-CAM-3) and full/complete occlusion (frame 1130, E-CAM-3) of an object; correct handling of object rotation (frame 2723, E-CAM-6) and inter-camera lighting variation.
- Intra-camera tracking (APF-MC) position-estimation error is shown (Fig. 4.13) to be consistently lower than color-based particle filter, color-based Mean-Shift, and contextual Mean-Shift baselines across a 100-frame test sequence (qualitative comparison chart, no single summary number given for the % improvement).
- A documented **false positive** case is shown (Fig. 5.14): a spurious re-identification alert lasting 2 frames, caused by camera motion/vibration (the testbed cameras were assumed static; camera-induced motion breaks the background model and can trigger false re-ID events).

## Stated limitations (from the document's own conclusion/limitations section)

- The proposed motion-detection chain is only robust for **static cameras** — it degrades under camera motion/vibration (explicitly identified as producing false positives, Fig. 5.14; robust background estimation under camera motion is listed as a top future-work item).
- The re-identification algorithm is vulnerable to **visual ambiguity between different objects with similar appearance** (e.g., two people wearing identical/near-identical clothing, as in retail/uniformed-worker settings) — the author explicitly names this as an unresolved weakness and proposes future integration of biometric (face) signals or spatio-temporal/confidence-weighted disambiguation as future work.
- The color-adaptation (MBTF) calibration step, while enabling decentralization, is flagged as a factor limiting scalability of the system — the author lists development of new inter-camera-invariant descriptors (rather than relying on the pairwise color-transfer calibration) as a specific future-work item.
- General/mono-object visual tracking is called out (in the thesis's own words) as "still a problem at the heart of research" — the author acknowledges that even after the improvements proposed in this thesis, tracking under arbitrary complex conditions remains only partially solved, and lists occlusion detection and principled data-association mechanisms as needed future improvements to the recursive filter.
- No mechanism for resolving decision ambiguity at the consensus level is built in beyond the basic protocol — explicitly flagged as future work (e.g., assigning trust/confidence weights to individual camera-agents).

## Claimed contributions (from the document itself)

1. A new motion-detection processing chain (GMM background modeling + Gaussian-kernel post-processing + connected-component labeling) improving robustness of foreground/background segmentation (own prior publication: Bousetouane 2011).
2. A new invariant/discriminant target-representation model mixing statistical moments and Haralick co-occurrence texture features for inter/intra-camera object description (Bousetouane 2012, 2013b).
3. A new adaptive mixed-state particle filter ("APF-MC") for intra-camera tracking that adapts particle search-space to real target size and integrates multiple visual cues in its likelihood model (Bousetouane 2013a).
4. **A fully decentralized, consensus-based algorithm for inter-camera object re-identification** in an ad-hoc, uncalibrated, disjoint-view camera network — claimed by the author as the first such fully decentralized solution in the literature to their knowledge (Bousetouane 2013c).
5. A generic multi-agent "cognitive camera" architecture integrating all of the above into per-camera autonomous agents that collaborate via consensus, deployed and validated on a real 7-camera testbed.

## Critical assessment (own analysis)

- **Genuine architectural relevance, but "agent" here means rule-based distributed decision logic, not reasoning/planning agents.** The multi-agent framing is well-earned architecturally (autonomous local processing + explicit inter-node consensus protocol), but each "agent" runs a fixed, hand-engineered pipeline with no learning, no planning, no natural-language reasoning, and no goal decomposition — it is closer to a distributed sensor-fusion/consensus system than to what "agentic AI" typically denotes today (LLM-driven reasoning, tool use, autonomous multi-step planning). This should be framed precisely when citing it as prior art: it establishes that "multi-camera + multi-agent decentralization" was already explored locally in 2015, but not "LLM-based reasoning agents for surveillance."
- **Evaluation scale is small and self-collected.** The core re-identification result (90.11% mean TPR) is drawn from only 4 scenarios with 6 total human subjects across a 7-camera network built by the author — there is no comparison against a standard public multi-camera re-ID benchmark (e.g., no use of established datasets like i-LIDS MCTS or similar for the actual re-ID chapter, only for the mono-camera tracking chapter). This limits external validity and makes the 90.11% figure hard to compare against contemporary/subsequent re-ID literature.
- **No deep learning** — expected given the 2015 timeframe and the "reconnaissance de formes" specialty framing, but it means the appearance model (color histograms + Haralick texture) is comparatively weak by current re-ID standards (modern re-ID relies on learned CNN/transformer embeddings) — the author's own acknowledged weakness (identical-clothing ambiguity) is a direct symptom of this.
- **Camera-motion fragility is a real deployment gap** for any surveillance system claiming robustness in "real-life" conditions — the thesis's own testbed assumes static cameras, and the one documented false-positive case in the thesis is directly attributable to this assumption breaking.
- **The consensus protocol's ambiguity-resolution is explicitly incomplete** — the thesis itself flags that visually similar objects (identical clothing) can break the decentralized identity-assignment logic, and that no confidence-weighting or biometric fallback is implemented, only proposed as future work.
- Overall, claims are calibrated reasonably modestly relative to their evidence — the author does not overclaim "solved" tracking/re-ID; the conclusion and perspectives sections are candid about remaining gaps (camera motion, ambiguity resolution, scalability of pairwise color calibration).

## Relevance note

This thesis is the single most architecturally relevant local prior-work document found so far for an "agentic multi-agent surveillance system" research direction, on the specific axis of **multi-camera coordination via genuinely autonomous, cooperating decision-making units** — it explicitly frames each camera as a "cognitive agent," implements a formal inter-agent consensus protocol for identity agreement, and validates it on a real multi-camera ad-hoc network, which is exactly the kind of "multi-agent architecture" and "multi-camera coordination" axis a new thesis would need to differentiate against. However, it is *not* relevant on the "autonomous reasoning agents," "LLM-based agents," or "explainable alerting" axes: there is no learning-based reasoning, no natural-language component, no planning/tool-use loop, and no anomaly-detection or explainable-alert generation — the "agents" perform a fixed classical-CV pipeline plus a hard-coded consensus rule. A new contribution could credibly position itself as extending this 2015 rule-based, hand-engineered multi-agent consensus paradigm toward learning-based / LLM-driven reasoning agents, richer (deep-learned) appearance models to fix the identical-clothing ambiguity this thesis itself flags, and explainable, semantically-grounded alerting — none of which this thesis attempts.
