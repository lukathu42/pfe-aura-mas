## Research Problem

The thesis addresses automatic license plate recognition (ALPR) for Algerian vehicles ("images minéralogiques" = license-plate images) under real-world image-quality problems: non-uniform illumination, noise (screws/bolts on the plate), shadows, and varying lighting conditions (day/night/light/dark). The framing chapter (Ch.1) situates this inside the broader field of "vidéosurveillance intelligente" (VSI / intelligent video surveillance, i.e. video analytics), of which the author identifies plate recognition as one specific sub-application among several (motion detection, tracking, object/behavior classification, face recognition, etc.).

## Objectives

- Build a license-plate localization, segmentation, and recognition pipeline specifically tuned to Algerian plates (which have a fixed 10-digit format: wilaya code, manufacture year, vehicle type, serial number).
- Make the pipeline robust to non-uniform lighting, noise, shadow, and the variable font styles used by Algerian plate manufacturers.
- Evaluate the three-stage pipeline (localization → segmentation → recognition) quantitatively on a self-collected image set.

## Proposed Approach (high level)

A classical (non-deep-learning) computer-vision pipeline:
1. **Preprocessing:** RGB→grayscale conversion, 3×3 median filter for noise reduction, Otsu binarization (local/dynamic threshold), Prewitt edge detection.
2. **Localization:** horizontal projection of the edge-detected binary image to find the plate's vertical position (the image is divided into 8 bands of 30px given a fixed 2m camera-to-vehicle distance; the band with most white pixels is assumed to hold the plate), then a fixed pixel-coordinate crop [85,240].
3. **Segmentation:** binarize + invert the cropped plate image, then run sequential 4-connectivity connected-component labeling; keep only components whose bounding box falls within an a-priori width/height range for Algerian plate digits, to filter out noise objects (e.g. screws).
4. **Recognition:** normalize each segmented character to 11×26 px, extract shape features, classify with multiclass SVM (one-vs-rest, RBF kernel, σ=1, C=100 found empirically best).

## System Architecture

**Single-module sequential pipeline**, not multi-agent and not truly multi-sensor "fusion" in the data-fusion sense (see Critical Assessment). Three sequential stages, each feeding the next:
`Image du véhicule → [Localisation: grayscale → median filter → Otsu binarization → Prewitt edges → horizontal projection] → [Segmentation: connected-component labeling + bounding-box filtering] → [Reconnaissance: SVM one-vs-rest] → résultat`

No feedback loops, no autonomous decision-making units, no cooperating agents, no multi-camera coordination. It is a deterministic, hand-tuned pipeline with fixed empirical thresholds (fixed 2m camera distance, fixed pixel band [85,240], fixed height/width ranges for character bounding boxes).

## AI/ML Techniques Used

- Support Vector Machines (SVM), multiclass via one-vs-rest (1vsR) decomposition, RBF and polynomial kernels compared; RBF (σ=1, C=100) selected.
- No neural networks are actually implemented in the proposed system (a literature-review section 2.4.2 discusses neural-net-based plate recognition from other papers, but the author's own system uses only SVM).

## Computer Vision Techniques

- Grayscale conversion (standard luminance formula), median filtering, Otsu thresholding/binarization, Prewitt edge detection, horizontal pixel-projection profiling, sequential connected-component (4-connexe) labeling with bounding-box shape filtering, SVM-based OCR-style character classification.

## Agent-based / Multi-agent / Distributed / Coordination Components

**None.** Despite the thesis specialty being "Informatique Répartie et Mobile" (Distributed & Mobile Computing) and the cover title containing "Fusion d'informations," the implemented system is a single-threaded, single-camera, single-machine deterministic pipeline with no autonomous agents, no distributed processing, no multi-camera coordination, and no genuine multi-source data fusion. The word "fusion" in the title is not substantiated by any fusion methodology (e.g., Dempster-Shafer, Bayesian fusion, multi-sensor fusion) found anywhere in the 103 pages read — see Critical Assessment.

## Dataset(s)

**Custom/collected, not a public benchmark.** 120 images of Algerian vehicles, 320×240 px, RGB, photographed by the author's team under two conditions: 60 "favorable" (uniform lighting) and 60 "unfavorable" (sun glare, shadow, low light, night, noise) — one image was later dropped from the unfavorable group after failed localization, leaving 119 usable plates for segmentation.

## Evaluation Methodology and Metrics

Simple accuracy/hit-rate at each of the three pipeline stages, computed separately for the two lighting-condition subsets:
- **Localization:** % of plates correctly cropped out of 120.
- **Segmentation:** % of plates whose characters were correctly isolated out of the 119 successfully localized plates.
- **Recognition:** % of plates whose full character sequence was correctly classified out of the 115 successfully segmented plates, tested across SVM hyperparameter sweeps (C = 50/80/90/100/200/500).

No cross-validation, no train/test split description beyond "base d'apprentissage" built from character images "in different fonts encountered on plates" (unspecified size), no comparison against other ALPR systems on a shared benchmark (there is no shared benchmark for Algerian plates), no confusion matrix beyond a qualitative note.

## Main Quantitative Results

| Stage | Favorable (n=60) | Unfavorable (n=59-60) | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Localization | 60/60 | 59/60 | 119/120 = **99.16%** |

## Critical Assessment (own analysis)

- **Title/content mismatch:** the cover page and library filename promise "information fusion for intelligent video surveillance," a phrase that in the surveillance/sensor literature normally denotes combining multiple sensors, cameras, or modalities (or multiple classifier outputs via a formal fusion rule such as Dempster-Shafer or Bayesian combination). Nothing of that kind appears anywhere in the 103 pages: it is a single-camera, single-modality, purely sequential three-stage pipeline. The only place multiple "classifiers" appear is the one-vs-rest SVM decomposition (multiple binary SVMs voting to decide one multiclass label) — a standard multiclass reduction technique, not information fusion in the surveillance-system sense. This looks like either a legacy/renamed thesis title or a mismatch between the registered project title and what was ultimately delivered.
- **Very small, non-benchmark, self-collected dataset** (120 images, one scene type: frontal plate captured at a fixed, controlled 2m distance for access-control gates). Results (91.3% recognition) cannot be meaningfully compared to public ALPR benchmarks and are unlikely to generalize to oblique angles, multiple plate distances, or non-access-control camera placements — a limitation the author's own future-work list implicitly concedes (camera-angle variation, plate tilt).
- **Dated, purely classical CV techniques** (Otsu, Prewitt, connected components, one hand-tuned pixel band) — reasonable for a 2015 Magister thesis but offers essentially no transferable methodology to a modern deep-learning or agentic system; the entire localization stage relies on a fixed camera distance assumption (2m) which is a strong, narrow deployment constraint.
- **No anomaly detection, no behavioral reasoning, no multi-camera or multi-agent architecture** anywhere in the document — its only substantive connection to "surveillance" is that ALPR is one classic sub-task of video analytics (as laid out in the Ch.1 literature survey), not that it advances autonomous or cooperative surveillance reasoning.
- The recognition accuracy denominator (115) mixes whole-plate correctness with per-character sub-results in a way that is not fully transparent in Table 4.3 (values such as "42/59" for a favorable subset suggest whole-plate, not per-character accuracy) — the reporting could be clearer about what unit is being measured.

## Relevance to "Agentic Multi-Agent Surveillance System" Direction

Minimal / tangential. The document belongs to the same broad academic umbrella (video-surveillance analytics, "vidéosurveillance intelligente" as framed in its own Ch.1 literature overview of VSI applications — banking, transport, retail, public sector, education) that a future agentic multi-agent surveillance thesis would also sit under, and it is useful as evidence of what "prior local work" in the department looks like: single-task, non-agentic, classical-CV pipelines with no cooperating decision-making components. Its only conceivable reuse value for an agentic MAS project would be as a candidate "tool" a future perception agent could invoke (an ALPR module) inside a larger cooperative architecture — but the thesis itself does not propose or gesture toward any such integration.
