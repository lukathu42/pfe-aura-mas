\chapter{State of the Art in Distributed and Agentic Surveillance}
\label{chap:sota}

\section{Introduction}

This chapter reviews the research areas used in AURA-MAS: distributed and edge video analytics, multi-agent and \gls{marl}-based surveillance, agentic multimodal alerting, and privacy and legal governance. It then positions the prototype against the gaps identified in this review.

\section{Distributed and Edge Video Analytics}

Bandwidth, latency, and privacy have moved more video analysis from cloud services to the edge \cite{do2025survey, toshpulatov2024edge}. NVIDIA DeepStream \cite{nvidia2024deepstream} shows multi-stream, hardware-accelerated inference at the edge. ONNX Runtime \cite{kacar2024onnx} and single-board computers make per-camera inference more practical \cite{sbc2024review}. Edge-cloud designs often filter easy cases locally and send ambiguous cases upward \cite{ghasemi2024edgecloudai}. AURA-MAS applies the same idea across agents: edge perception, site coordination, then explanation. Distributed-surveillance studies also report benefits in elasticity and fault containment \cite{sada2019distributed, karim2024real}.

On the communication substrate, comparative studies of \gls{iot} messaging protocols consistently find \gls{mqtt} superior for constrained, event-driven workloads \cite{zhang2024comparison, almanasrah2026message, arafat2025nextgen}, with Mosquitto as the standard broker \cite{light2017mosquitto} and \gls{qos} levels providing per-topic delivery guarantees. Real-time alert pipelines increasingly pair a pub/sub bus with a durable stream store (Redis Streams / Kafka) for alerts and audit \cite{melancon2026blazeaiot, gulotta2023realtime, yoshino2025high}. AURA-MAS adopts exactly this two-tier substrate.

\section{Multi-Agent and Learning-Based Surveillance}

Classical multi-camera coordination research addresses camera hand-off, field-of-view negotiation, and distributed tracking \cite{amosa2023multicamera, sanmiguel2022detection}. Task allocation via market mechanisms remains the practical workhorse \cite{smith1980contract, parker2002alliance}, with modern variants addressing task-oriented communication under bandwidth constraints \cite{zhang2026taskoriented} and submodular sensor selection \cite{liu2026submodular}.

\gls{marl} is also used for cooperative sensing. Multi-UAV search \cite{zheng2026multiuav}, target coverage \cite{yang2025multiagent}, and related coverage formulations \cite{wang2026improved} show that learned policies can outperform hand-crafted coordination in simulation. QMIX \cite{rashid2018qmix} and MADDPG \cite{lowe2017multiagent} are common algorithms, with PettingZoo \cite{terry2021pettingzoo} and RLlib \cite{liang2018rllib} supporting experimentation. Real surveillance deployments remain scarce. Sample complexity, sim-to-real transfer, and limited auditability make these methods difficult to use in safety-critical settings. AURA-MAS therefore uses an auction that can be inspected directly.

\section{Agentic and Multimodal Alerting}

Recent work applies \gls{llm}/\gls{vlm} agents to video understanding. Hawk \cite{cheng2025hawk} and VideoAgent \cite{fan2024videoagent} use an \gls{llm} planner to orchestrate perception tools. AVadCLIP \cite{wu2025avadclip} and related methods \cite{ustun2026multimodal} combine audio and video for anomaly detection. Other work uses description-based \gls{sed} \cite{nishida2026description} or explainable \gls{vad} \cite{huang2025exvad, alkahtani2026explainable} to make outputs easier to interpret. AutoGen \cite{wu2023autogen} and LangGraph \cite{langgraph2024} provide orchestration machinery, while \cite{sapkota2025ai, fan2024survey, duan2024exploration} review multi-agent \gls{llm} frameworks. Guardrails \cite{rebedea2023nemo} and LLM-as-judge evaluation \cite{zheng2023judging} can reduce hallucination, but do not remove it. In the systems reviewed here, the generative component is not architecturally separated from the alert decision; it is either part of detection or an unchecked narrator.

\section{Privacy, Ethics, and Legal Governance}

Surveillance AI is now heavily regulated territory. The \gls{gdpr} imposes lawfulness, data minimization, and storage limitation on video processing \cite{gdpr2016}; European guidance discourages centralized retention of identifiable footage \cite{cnil2025reduced, iapp2022smartcameras}. The EU AI Act \cite{euaiact2024} prohibits real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces (with narrow exceptions) and classifies most other surveillance AI as high-risk, mandating risk management, logging, human oversight, and transparency \cite{barkane2022questioning, barkane2025prohibited, edri2024biometric, lexology2025augmented}. Trustworthy-AI frameworks (NIST AI RMF \cite{livingsecurity2026nist}, reviewed in \cite{forasoft2026trustworthy, ruf2021demystifying}) converge on the same operational requirements: edge-local processing, anonymization, audit trails, and human-in-the-loop decision authority. These requirements are \emph{constitutive} design constraints for AURA-MAS, not afterthoughts.

This review identifies three gaps. \textbf{(G1)} None of the surveyed systems combines auditable multi-agent coordination with modern zero-shot perception. \textbf{(G2)} The surveyed agentic explanation layers are not separated from decision-making and grounded by construction. \textbf{(G3)} Academic work usually evaluates models rather than complete systems with time-to-alert, false alerts per hour, and coordination overhead under a controlled ablation. AURA-MAS addresses these points at prototype scale and within explicit legal constraints.

\section{Conclusion}

The state of the art provides mature components and clear regulatory constraints but no integrated, auditable, privacy-preserving multi-agent architecture with grounded agentic explanation. The next chapter presents our design.
