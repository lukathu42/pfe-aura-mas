# Multi-Agent Intelligent Surveillance Thesis Blueprint

## Executive summary

The strongest and most defensible MSc thesis direction is **not** “surveillance with AI” in the abstract, but a **privacy-aware, edge-first, hierarchical multi-agent system** for a **semi-structured site** such as a warehouse, logistics yard, industrial facility, or campus zone. That framing is modern for three reasons. First, recent work in distributed video analytics increasingly favors cloud-edge-terminal or edge-first architectures because they reduce latency, distribute computation, and can improve privacy by keeping raw sensor data local. Second, recent surveillance research has moved beyond single-camera anomaly detection toward multi-camera tracking, multimodal understanding, and richer event semantics. Third, “agentic AI” is now better understood as **stateful orchestration among specialized agents** rather than a monolithic model pretending to do everything. citeturn15search0turn37view1turn28view8turn28view9turn29view3turn29view7turn32view4

For a PFE, the **recommended contribution** is a system that combines: **local perception agents** at the sensor/edge layer, **fusion and coordination agents** at the site layer, **rule-guarded agentic reasoning** for alert validation and explanation, and **simulation-driven evaluation** for multi-agent coordination. The thesis should compare a conventional centralized baseline against a hierarchical multi-agent design, then add one coordination mechanism—preferably **auction/consensus** for robustness and interpretability, with **MAPPO** or **QMIX** as a simulation-only advanced track if time permits. citeturn37view2turn37view3turn38view0turn12search0turn10search0turn10search12

A realistic implementation stack is: **ROS 2 + DDS** for real-time inter-agent communication, **Kafka** or MQTT for event streaming and lightweight telemetry, **YOLO-class detectors + ByteTrack/BoT-SORT** for object perception, **MMAction2** for event recognition, **MMPose** for pose cues, **VadCLIP** or a similar recent weakly supervised anomaly detector for surveillance anomalies, **AST/BEATs-class** models for audio anomaly detection, and **CARLA**, **AirSim**, or **Isaac Sim / Omniverse Replicator** for synthetic data and coordination experiments. citeturn31view0turn28view3turn32view0turn32view1turn33view3turn33view4turn29view5turn29view6turn38view3turn38view2turn23search1turn28view4turn36view2turn29view9turn36view0turn36view1turn36view3

Because the legal and ethical context around algorithmic video analysis is tightening, the thesis should explicitly exclude or strictly de-emphasize public-space biometric identification, and instead prioritize **non-biometric event detection**, **on-edge anonymization**, **data minimization**, **human-in-the-loop escalation**, and **auditability**. That makes the work more publishable, more deployable, and more aligned with current guidance from the entity["organization","European Commission","eu executive body"], entity["organization","CNIL","french privacy regulator"], entity["organization","ANSSI","french cyber agency"], and entity["organization","NIST","us standards agency"]. citeturn28view0turn34view0turn34view1turn28view1turn34view2turn34view3turn34view4turn34view5turn35view1turn35view2

### Preferred project positioning

For a master’s thesis, the best application context is a **semi-closed operational site**—warehouse, smart building, industrial perimeter, or campus logistics zone—rather than generic citywide public surveillance. This preserves the research value of multi-camera coordination and alerting, while lowering legal complexity and making synthetic digital twins much more realistic. ## Research problem and evaluation design

### Problem statement

Most intelligent surveillance systems remain **camera-siloed, centralized, and reactive**: they detect events after they happen, struggle to fuse heterogeneous evidence across cameras and microphones, and generate too many false alerts for operators. At the same time, current “agentic AI” discourse often overfocuses on LLM orchestration and under-specifies the hard parts of perception, synchronization, task allocation, and governance. The research gap is therefore a **rigorously evaluated multi-agent surveillance architecture** in which specialized agents cooperate to perceive a site, form a shared situational picture, allocate sensing or verification tasks, and emit timely, auditable alerts under realistic latency and privacy constraints. citeturn15search0turn37view1turn29view3turn30search18turn29view7

### Research questions, hypotheses, and measurable objectives

| Research question | Hypothesis | Measurable objective | Primary metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does a hierarchical multi-agent architecture outperform a conventional centralized pipeline for site monitoring? | A hierarchical design will reduce end-to-end alert latency and improve robustness under multi-stream load because computation is distributed and evidence is fused at the site layer rather than after raw transport. | Demonstrate lower mean time-to-alert and equal or better event-level F1 than a centralized baseline. | Mean time-to-alert, end-to-end event F1, throughput, dropped-frame rate, bandwidth per camera. citeturn15search0turn37view1turn28view3 |
| Does multimodal fusion outperform vision-only monitoring in hard conditions? | Audio-visual and cross-camera fusion will improve precision/recall under occlusion, low resolution, bad viewing angles, or scene ambiguity. | Show statistically meaningful gains over vision-only baselines on at least one audio-visual anomaly benchmark and one multi-camera benchmark. | AUC/AP/F1 for anomaly detection, pAUC for audio ASD, HOTA/IDF1 for tracking. citeturn29view2turn31view3turn31view4turn16search0turn31view5 |
| Which coordination mechanism is most appropriate for a PFE: rules, auction/consensus, or MARL? | Auction/consensus methods will be easier to validate and explain, while MAPPO/QMIX may deliver better simulated coordination under richer active-sensing dynamics. | Compare a rule-based scheduler, an auction/consensus scheduler, and one MARL scheduler in simulation. | Task completion rate, target coverage, coordination overhead, alert timeliness, resource utilization. citeturn10search0turn10search12turn37view2turn37view3turn38view0turn12search0 |
| Can agentic reasoning improve alert quality without becoming a safety risk? | A rule-guarded agentic layer can improve operator usability by producing richer, evidence-linked summaries, but final escalation should remain policy-bound and auditable. | Produce structured alerts with evidence traces; measure operator-oriented explanation quality. | Evidence completeness, alert justification coverage, contradiction rate, human-rated usefulness. citeturn29view7turn32view3turn32view4turn35view1 |

### Practical success criteria

A strong PFE should aim for the following **design targets** rather than treat them as guaranteed benchmark numbers:

| Objective | Suggested target |
|---|---|
| Event detection quality | Competitive with modern weakly supervised anomaly baselines on UCF-Crime / XD-Violence, or a clear improvement over your centralized baseline |
| Tracking quality | Meaningful HOTA and IDF1 gains from cross-camera fusion over per-camera tracking |
| Alert speed | Sub-second to low-single-digit-second latency in the demo pipeline |
| False alert rate | Explicitly reported as false alerts per hour or per scene |
| Privacy robustness | Raw identity-bearing frames retained only when strictly necessary; anonymized evidence paths by default |
| Reproducibility | One-command experiment configs, data versioning, tracked metrics, reproducible evaluation scripts |

These criteria match the standard metric families used in surveillance anomaly detection, tracking, object detection, and DCASE-style audio anomaly scoring. citeturn30search7turn29view2turn16search0turn31view5turn17search0turn17search1turn16search3turn31view4

## Proposed multi-agent architecture

### Architectural principle

The recommended design is a **hierarchical, event-driven MAS** with three layers:

1. **Sensor/edge layer** for low-latency local inference.
2. **Site coordination layer** for fusion, scheduling, and risk estimation.
3. **Operator/governance layer** for alert validation, audit logging, and human oversight.

This design follows the broader trajectory of edge video analytics and cloud-edge-terminal collaboration, while staying faithful to practical real-time robotics and distributed systems tooling. citeturn15search0turn37view1turn28view3turn31view0

### Coordination algorithm comparison

| Family | Best use case | Advantages | Drawbacks | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rule-based finite-state scheduler | First integrated prototype | Easy to validate, strong safety, reproducible | Brittle under complex trade-offs | Mandatory baseline |
| Consensus-based coordination | Distributed agreement on risk or coverage maps | Interpretable, decentralized, good for periodic state sharing | Limited expressiveness for richer task allocation | Strong option |
| Auction-based task allocation | PTZ/drone/camera assignment to suspicious targets or zones | Interpretable, modular, practical for limited resources | Requires well-designed bid functions | **Best classical choice for the recommended thesis** |
| MAPPO | Cooperative task allocation and active sensing in simulation | Strong empirical cooperative MARL baseline; good ecosystem support | Training instability and engineering cost | Best learned-coordination option |
| QMIX | Cooperative discrete decision spaces | CTDE-friendly; good for discrete sensor-task choices | Monotonicity assumption can constrain policies | Useful if action space is discrete |
| MADDPG | Continuous control of PTZ/drone behaviors | Handles continuous actions | Older, more complex, can be unstable | Use only if direct continuous control is central |

The literature supports communication-aware cooperative MARL as important, and MAPPO remains one of the strongest practical baselines. For a PFE, however, classical auction/consensus methods generally offer a better reproducibility-to-novelty ratio. citeturn37view2turn37view3turn38view0turn12search0turn38view1turn10search0turn10search12

### Perception, fusion, and anomaly modeling

A sound perception stack for this thesis should combine **object-centric**, **activity-centric**, and **scene-centric** views of the environment. In practice, that means object detection and tracking for “what is where,” action recognition and pose for “what people are doing,” and anomaly scoring for “what is unusual in this scene.” Recent surveillance research also supports adding language grounding or weakly supervised anomaly detection to improve semantic expressiveness. citeturn30search7turn29view2turn29view3turn27search4turn27search1

The best design choice is **hybrid multimodal fusion**:
- **Early fusion** when synchronized low-level signals are stable and calibration is strong.
- **Late fusion** when modalities have different sampling rates or reliability profiles.
- **Hybrid/gated fusion** when you want the model to down-weight unreliable modalities such as noisy microphones or occluded cameras.

For an MSc project, a very practical pattern is: **late fusion at alert time, plus track-level fusion in the world model**. That is much easier to debug than end-to-end cross-modal transformers across all agents. citeturn24search16turn24search19turn15search0

### Privacy, security, and governance requirements

The privacy-safe version of this thesis should **avoid biometric identification as a core contribution** and instead focus on **event detection, intrusion, abandoned-object, crowding, fight/fall/smoke/fire proxies, and anomalous machine sound or movement**. The Commission’s AI Act guidance highlights especially sensitive uses such as real-time remote biometric identification, and the CNIL emphasizes the intrusiveness of real-time algorithmic image analysis and the need for proportionality, transparency, DPIA-style analysis, and security controls. ROS 2 security, DDS security, and a risk-management overlay such as AI RMF give you the engineering side of that governance story. citeturn34view0turn34view1turn28view1turn34view2turn34view3turn34view5turn31view0turn35view1turn35view2

A robust governance design for the thesis should therefore include: **on-edge anonymization before export**, **role-based evidence access**, **encrypted transport**, **retention limits**, **operator acknowledgment**, **full audit logs**, and **clear separation between model inference, policy decision, and human action**. citeturn31view0turn32view0turn32view1turn35view1turn34view5

## Implementation and experimental plan

### Training and MLOps pipeline

A reproducible training pipeline should use **Hydra** for hierarchical experiment configuration, **DVC** for data and experiment tracking, and **MLflow** for model lineage and registry. That combination is unusually suitable for an academic thesis because it supports reproducibility without forcing you into one cloud vendor. citeturn33view2turn33view1turn33view0

A strong implementation plan is:

| Phase | What to do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Data preparation | Normalize labels, create train/val/test splits, add scenario metadata, define camera-map and zone schemas | Reproducible dataset manifests |
| Baseline perception training | Train or fine-tune detector, tracker, anomaly model, audio branch | Component baselines |
| Synthetic scenario generation | Script critical events such as intrusion, crowding, abandoned object, fight proxy, smoke/fire proxy, machine anomaly, and drone verification missions | Digital-twin scenarios and synthetic labeled data |
| Coordination learning / scheduling | Implement rule-based scheduler first, then auction/consensus, then optional MARL | Coordination benchmark |

| No cross-camera fusion vs fusion-enabled world model | Quantifies coordination effect |
| Rule scheduler vs auction/consensus vs MARL | Isolates the coordination contribution |
| No explanation agent vs explanation agent | Measures the usefulness of agentic summarization |
| Raw export vs anonymized evidence export | Demonstrates privacy-aware design trade-off |

### Evaluation methodology

A rigorous evaluation should separate **component-level**, **coordination-level**, and **system-level** performance.

**Component-level evaluation** should use benchmark-native metrics:
- Object detection: COCO-style AP / AP50 / AP75. citeturn17search0turn17search1
- Tracking: HOTA, IDF1, MOTA via TrackEval. citeturn16search0turn31view5
- Video anomaly detection: AUC/AP/F1 depending benchmark protocol. citeturn30search7turn29view2turn27search4
- Audio anomaly detection: AUC, pAUC, precision, recall, F1 using the official DCASE evaluator. citeturn16search3turn31view4

**Coordination-level evaluation** should measure:
- Average time from first suspicious cue to alert,
- Resource utilization by PTZ/drone/mobile agents,
- Coverage of suspicious zones and targets,
- Communication overhead and dropped assignments,
- Task success rate under partial observability.  
These are system design metrics, so you should define them explicitly in the thesis methodology and evaluate them via simulation and scripted demos. The PETtingZoo/RLlib stack is well suited for this. citeturn28view6turn28view7turn37view2

**System-level evaluation** should include:
- false alerts per hour,
- missed-event rate,
- operator acknowledgment time,
- audit completeness,
- anonymization success rate,
- degradation under occlusion, bad lighting, microphone noise, and packet loss.

## Risks, ethics, and mitigation

### Main risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Excessive scope | Surveillance, multimodality, simulation, MARL, and governance can easily become too large for one PFE | Freeze a core path: hierarchical MAS + one real anomaly benchmark + one synthetic coordination setting |
| Domain shift | Public datasets and synthetic scenes may not match the target site | Report this explicitly; use synthetic-to-real evaluation and scenario-based robustness tests |
| False alarms and missed events | Operator distrust can kill the usefulness of the system | Calibrate thresholds, report false alerts per hour, add evidence bundles and operator confirmations |
| LLM/agent hallucination | Agentic summaries can invent causes or evidence | Keep the explanation agent downstream of rule-based alert validation; only summarize grounded evidence |
| Privacy intrusion | Raw video export and identity correlation are sensitive | Default to edge inference, anonymized evidence, retention limits, and non-biometric research goals |
| Security exposure | Compromised agents or message buses can leak evidence or inject events | Use ROS 2 security, DDS enclaves, TLS, role-based access, durable audit logs |
| Regulatory non-compliance | Public-space analytics and worker monitoring can trigger strong legal constraints | Frame the thesis around semi-closed sites, proportionality, transparency, DPIA-style analysis, and current AI/GDPR guidance |

These mitigations align with current AI risk-management principles, CNIL recommendations, and the AI Act’s risk-based logic. citeturn35view1turn35view2turn34view0turn34view1turn28view1turn34view5turn31view0

### Ethical position for the thesis

The ethically strongest framing is: **augment human operators with timely, evidence-linked alerts**, rather than automate punishment, identity inference, or autonomous intervention. In the report, explicitly state that the system is for **decision support**, not fully autonomous enforcement. That positioning is more consistent with trustworthy AI principles, privacy law, and current European guidance on sensitive surveillance uses. citeturn35view1turn35view2turn34view1turn28view1

### Open questions and limitations

Three limitations should be acknowledged explicitly in the thesis. First, no public benchmark perfectly represents a real industrial or campus deployment, so external validity will remain partial. Second, if you do not have access to private site data, the thesis will rely on public and synthetic datasets, which is acceptable but should be stated clearly. Third, any use of identity or biometrics should be excluded or tightly constrained, because the most legally and ethically sensitive areas of video analytics are exactly where public scrutiny is increasing. citeturn28view8turn28view9turn29view3turn34view1turn28view1

The most balanced final thesis proposition is therefore:

**Build a privacy-aware hierarchical multi-agent surveillance system for a semi-structured site, benchmark its perception stack on public surveillance and audio datasets, evaluate its coordination logic in simulation, and show that agentic orchestration improves alert usability only when constrained by deterministic policy rules and human oversight.** citeturn15search0turn37view1turn29view3turn31view4turn37view3turn29view7turn35view2
